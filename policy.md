# Progress Policy Summary

## Public Overview (October 2025)

This is a short, public-facing overview of Progress’ priorities for the 2029 General Election and preceding local contests. It summarises goals and direction; detailed implementation notes are kept internal or in the full policy pack.

It remains a work-in-progress as of October 2025.

---

## The Big Three

### 1) Economy — higher wages, lower prices, faster growth

Our goal: make Britain the easiest place in the world to build world‑class companies and careers, while driving down the cost of living.

- E1. Britain, the world’s largest “unicorn farm”: use smarter procurement, targeted investment, and pro‑founder rules to scale British tech firms at home instead of exporting them.
- E1+. Unlock capital for innovation: create simple routes for private wealth to back British technology and IPOs, shifting investment from idle assets into productive growth.
- E2. Prosperity Zones (SEZs): red‑hot regional hubs with world‑class planning, tax and skills support to re‑industrialise the Midlands, North, Scotland, Wales and NI.
- E3. The nation of shopkeepers: make starting and growing a small business radically simpler (e.g., “Starter Ltd”, lighter reporting, fair tax for micro‑firms; clean up IR35 friction).
- E4. Cost of living: tear down needless costs in energy, food, and logistics; reward innovation that cuts household bills.
- E5. Skill capital: a national push for apprenticeships, technical colleges and on‑the‑job training so people earn more, sooner.
- E6. Paid overtime and partnership: spread employee ownership and profit‑sharing so workers gain when firms succeed.
- E7. Dual‑mandate unions: align worker voice with long‑term company health and productivity.
- E8. Friendly societies: revive community mutuals for saving, insurance and local investment with simple, supportive rules.

Selected SEZ focus areas (illustrative): Advanced manufacturing (Midlands), Life sciences (Golden Triangle), Genomics (Scotland), Geo‑engineering (Teesside), Space (Cornwall/Scotland), Defence tech (Cheltenham), AI logistics (Merseyside/Southampton), Semiconductors (Newport).

### 2) Crime — safer streets, cleaner institutions, open justice

Our goal: cut crime dramatically and restore trust through transparency and relentless focus on repeat and serious offenders.

- C1. Open justice: make crime data, court outputs and oversight radically more transparent while respecting victim privacy.
- C2. Zero‑Crime Britain (direction of travel): expand capacity, focus on violent/sexual and organised crime, and deter repeat offending with clear, consistent consequences.
- White‑collar crime: a standing anti‑corruption capability focused on procurement abuse, fraud and public‑office corruption.
- Unified justice (case study Scotland): move toward integrated, digital, end‑to‑end services that get cases heard faster and more consistently across the system.

### 3) Civil Service Reform — the world’s most high‑performance public service

Our goal: deliver more, waste less, and put mission before bureaucracy.

- Energy: lowest industrial energy costs in the G7 by 2035 through massive build, smarter regulation and grid efficiency.
- NHS: back to basics on acute care; modernise data and procurement; cut paperwork, grow clinical capacity and frontline pay; integrate with social care pathways.
- Planning and procurement: clear the blockers that make building and buying slow, complex and expensive.
- People and culture: higher standards, clearer accountability, and pay aligned with national outcomes.
- Support that works: better pathways for disabled citizens, veterans, new mums and early years—simple access, faster help.

## Economy policies (public summary)

### E1. Britain, the world’s largest unicorn farm
Goal: keep our best companies British as they scale.

- Use government procurement and targeted funds to de‑risk early markets for UK startups.
- Speed up approvals and reduce red tape for high‑growth sectors (AI, healthtech, fintech, climate tech).
- Make employee ownership and stock options simpler and fairer.
- Make it easier to bring in top talent where skills are scarce.

### E1+. Funding technology with private capital
Goal: move more domestic capital into productive British innovation.

- Create simple, time‑bound routes for private investors to reinvest gains into UK tech and listings.
- Crowd‑in institutional money alongside private capital to scale winners at home.

### E2. Prosperity Zones (SEZs)
Goal: re‑industrialise regions with specialist hubs.

- World‑class planning and infrastructure, embedded skills pipelines, and pro‑build rules.
- Long‑term capital and international investment partnerships.
- Local “sandpits” to pilot smarter rules and technology safely.

### E3. Britain, the nation of shopkeepers
Goal: unlock millions of small British enterprises.

- “Starter Ltd”: a simple micro‑company category (low‑cost setup, minimal reporting, easy graduation).
- Fairer treatment for freelancers and first hires; simpler VAT and dividend rules for small businesses.
- Lower business rates pressure on high streets.

### E4. Driving down the cost of living
Goal: structurally cheaper essentials.

- Cut duplication and compliance costs along food and manufacturing supply chains.
- Lower energy input costs for producers and logistics.
- Back agri‑tech and automation so British farming thrives while prices fall.

### E5. Deep focus on skill capital
Goal: every worker, every year, learns something valuable.

- Scale apprenticeships and technical colleges; expand employer‑led training.
- Personal “Skills Wallets” to accelerate upskilling.
- Early pathways from school into paid, skilled work.

### E6. Paid overtime and employee partnership
Goal: link reward to contribution across the economy.

- Encourage profit‑sharing, ESOPs and performance‑linked rewards.
- Protect paid overtime in sectors where unmanaged hours are common.

### E7. Dual‑mandate unions
Goal: worker voice aligned with long‑term company success.

- Strengthen cooperation on productivity, training and automation transitions.

### E8. The Friendly Society
Goal: rebuild local financial mutuals.

- Simple legal forms, light reporting, and prudent protections for community saving and insurance.

### Beyond the flagship — Economy

- E9. Space policy: back a national flagship human‑spaceflight project and UK space industry participation; modernise rules with allies.
- E10. Student debt relief: end interest on existing student loan repayments to help people move forward faster.

## Crime policies (public summary)

### C1. Championing open justice
Goal: sunlight, accountability and trust.

- Publish clearer, faster crime and court information; improve access to outcomes and benchmarks.

### C2. Zero‑Crime Britain (direction)
Goal: fewer victims, fewer repeat offenders.

- Expand capacity where it cuts harm most; prioritise violent, sexual, organised and prolific offending.
- Stronger whistleblower protections to expose fraud and abuse.
- Tougher, cleaner public‑sector and corporate governance.

### C2+. Unified judicial services (Scotland pilot)
Goal: faster, simpler, digital end‑to‑end justice.

- Streamlined tiers, shared data, consistent standards; scale nationally if successful.

## Civil Service Reform (public summary)

### Energy
Goal: the cheapest reliable power in Europe by the mid‑2030s.

- Build baseload, fix market distortions, unlock grid capacity, and reduce waste.

### The NHS
Goal: world‑class acute care, modern data and procurement, and more time for patients.

- Cut busywork, grow clinical headcount, modernise systems, and integrate with social care to free beds.

### Planning and procurement
Goal: build faster, buy smarter.

- Streamline approvals, standardise what works, and reduce costly duplication.

### People and services
Goal: high standards with humane support.

- Clear accountability, performance‑linked rewards, and simple access to support for disabled citizens, veterans, new mums and early years.

## Education and Research

### EDU1. A freer, higher‑trust research culture
Goal: back bold, long‑horizon science.

- Fund novel work with fewer box‑ticking constraints and more trust in researchers.
- Build a leadership cadre able to bridge labs, industry and policy.

### EDU2. Affordable academic materials
Goal: cheaper to publish, cheaper to read.

- Shift institutions toward fair‑price, public‑access models and end monopoly pricing.
