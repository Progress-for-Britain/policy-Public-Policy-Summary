### E1. Britain, the World’s Largest Unicorn Farm

Our economic regeneration plan is based partly on building up Britain’s entrepreneurial economy, so that it produces more £1-billion-valued startup companies (pound-for-pound) than any other country or region in the world.

- Reduced capital gains tax for investments in key sectors

### E1+. A Vision of Unicorn Farming

*By Damian Horton*

If you ran a poll on the UK’s ability to create exciting world-leading start-ups, the general view would be that we’re punching above our weight globally. More informed people might reel off several great examples, such as DeepMind, ARM and Revolut, as evidence of our reasonable global position.

The reality is much more depressing. DeepMind, whilst British founded, was long-ago acquired by Google/Alphabet. ARM was acquired by Softbank and then re-listed in the United States. Revolut has already indicated that it won’t seek a UK Initial Public Offering (IPO), hinting that it’s future will also be as a foreign company[1].

These are just the top examples. Lower down, things look worse. Most of the current crop of UK tech unicorns run huge losses on a level of revenue that places them closer to an SME (small or medium sized business) than a potential FTSE 100 constituent or world-beater. The handful of fintech banks, such as Monzo or Starling, are similar to Skipton Building Society (£500m revenue) and far away from JP Morgan. It’s worth noting that Revolut, one of the few tech unicorn breakouts, didn’t obtain a UK banking license and much of its revenue comes from non-banking products or services.

### E1+. Funding Technology and Innovation with Property

*By Damian Horton*

This case-study is based on the following assumptions:

- We are chronically short of equity investment for promising technology start-ups
- The UK is heavily overweight property as an investment
- Capital in investment properties is trapped due to Capital Gains Tax

To highlight the difference between technology investments and property investments, the UK financial sector has only raised £160m so far in 2025:

<https://www.ft.com/content/cb374a44-da84-4144-9a87-5180bd481696>

At the same time, the UK has accumulated well past £1 trillion in wealth from investment properties. The Resolution Foundation had this figure at £1 trillion in 2016 with the market increasing a further 50% since then:

<https://www.resolutionfoundation.org/app/uploads/2019/06/Game-of-Homes.pdf>

However much of the investment property is now trapped. Over the years the investment property prices have doubled or even tripled, and this leads to an unattractive to sell for mostly older Baby Boomer owners. With prices on an investment property doubling and with a higher rate capital gains tax of 24%, the property investor would pay away 12% in capital gains tax if the property were to be sold (after house prices doubling). If house prices had tripled, it would be 16% of total proceeds without). This is assuming no borrowing or leverage.

### E2. Prosperity Zones (SEZ), or “The North Gets Its Own Back”

We will designate several autonomous zones in the Midlands, North, Scotland, Wales & Northern Ireland as special economic zones, or ‘Prosperity Zones’. In these zones, industrial and metropolitan development will be hyper-incentivised and heavily capitalised. The aim is to turn these zones, each red hot and dedicated to specific specialist industrial sectors – such as aerospace, R&D, energy, small nuclear technology, pharmaceuticals etc. – into economic powerhouses. We will re-industrialise the nation and return pride, identity, and prosperity to those regions of Britain, from Grimsby to Broadhaven and from Carlisle to Coventry – gone so long without either.

- Advanced Manufacturing and Robotics Zone (Midlands) – Building on our strong precision engineering & automotive sector (Rolls-Royce, Jaguar) to build a world-class hub for robotics/automation/EV battery development, producing high-performance EVs, solid-state batteries, and hydrogen-electric hybrids to rival China’s lead in these fields.
- Genomics/Precision Medicine Zone (Scotland) Turning our existing record in genome sequencing into breakthroughs in gene therapies & personalised medicine, and other work across biopharma

### E3. Driving Down the Cost of Living

For Britain's most disadvantaged, excessive bureaucracy inflates costs, with up to 40% of purchasing power lost to administrative inefficiencies – that makes us all poorer. We will dismantle barriers, slash import costs, and negotiate advanced trade agreements to ensure affordability for all, especially those who need it most. We will torpedo every system which facilitates the cost-of-living crisis, making basic amenities and products far cheaper than they are presently.

- Incentives for decentralised manufacturing hubs to reduce prices
- Revision of energy regulations on manufacturing plants to reduce factory compliance costs, including energy subsidy [?]

### E4. Deep Focus on Building Skill Capital

We will make training and the providing of skills our highest priority. There is no surer way to give a person tracks to prosperity than to make sure they can do things this year they couldn’t last year. Employers will be incentivised to guarantee extensive on-the-job training, allowing for more work straight-out-of-school, and greater ease of career shifting. Every worker will, for the first 15 years of their career, be trained and assisted to receive at least one new Skill Badge each year. We will open up new technical colleges to expand vocational training capability for both 16-year-olds and mature students. Salaries and life prospects will soar.

- Emulate Germany’s dual-training model to fill 50% of vacancies via apprenticeships
- Setting up of SEZ-specialised ‘Miniversities’ focusing on embedded training initiatives, apprenticeships, work-study programs, and AI-adaptive learning, stimulated by tax breaks for companies proportionate to the number of domestic employees they see through training to permanent positions [E3]

### E5. Return of Paid Overtime and Employee Partnership Schemes

To ensure that employees receive a reasonable share in the profits they generate for their companies, we shall enforce rules on paid overtime, industry-sector-agnostic, and encourage companies to make their employees partners in business; if they help the company do well, they shall do well themselves. Greater loyalty to companies means deeper community ties and a stronger feeling of job- and life-security for everyone, while companies benefit through lower employee-churn costs, team loyalties, and stronger maintained knowledge traditions.

- ESOP – Employee Stock Ownership Plans, representative of a portion of company shares that increase in value based on the company’s performance and employee tenure, and which are paid out as dividends annually, are extended across the economy
- [Remainder Available in Full Policy Pack]

### E6. Dual-Mandate Unions

We believe that successful companies that grow our economy benefit us all. We also believe that all employees must be entitled to a fair share for their labour. To that end, we will restructure labour union organisation, so that union heads have mutual responsibility to both members and to the health of the companies they work for. The worker cannot be taken advantage of by the corporation; but nor can the corporation be allowed to be crippled by opportunistic actions on the part of the union.

- As in Germany’s "co-determination" model, union heads have board seats or equivalent positions with a company [?], having undergone business training in finance, strategy, and risk management
- Union leadership compensation is tied to company health, not worker dues, so that union heads are incentivised to think like business partners, not adversaries

### E7. The Friendly Society

Friendly societies, sometimes referred to otherwise as benefit societies, or mutual aid societies. These are mutual associations for the purposes of insurance, pensions, savings or cooperative banking. It is formed of a body of people who join together for a common financial or social purpose. We propose making these relatively tax exempt, encouraging community cohesion and intra-communal investment.

- Create online platforms for people to easily join, manage, and track their financial and social investments in a society
- "Social savings" systems, wherein people can save and pool resources together for specific social causes (e.g., environmental sustainability, educational grants, community projects).

#### E8. Space Policy

The space sectors of the US and China are experiencing rapid, transformative growth that is set to take humans to the Moon and Mars within a decade. If we continue as we are, we will be left behind and excluded from the table when it comes to dividing up the resources of the solar system and setting the rules of the interplanetary order. We will enter the 22nd century as a third tier power and lose talent to countries that are expanding into the new frontier. We need a space strategy that builds on our existing strengths, and also prepares for the challenges ahead.

- We should commit to one, long term, unifying project which will provide predictable contracts for British companies of all scales.
- We recommend for this project the construction of a large, rotating habitat in Earth orbit as this project, as it allows cheaper access and thus more people able to travel there. It will be effectively a new overseas territory, and the base for our future space endeavours.

#### E9. Student Debt Relief

There is no greater or more direct relief from poverty, nor any more open channel to success, than being able to participate in free, dynamic markets. Debt is a direct impediment to this kind of market participation. To this end, Progress acknowledges the extraordinary depressive weight decades of academic inflation and rampant credentialism has had on not just the employment prospects/professional competitiveness of two generations of Britons, but on the collective debt burden as well.

With this in mind, Progress will absolve those with outstanding student debts of the duty of paying interest on debt repayments.

### C2+. Unified Judicial Services

**Scotland as case-study**

Despite Scotland having disproportionately higher spending on its justice system (up to 2x OECD norms by measurements), it is not yielding results. This is due to structural problems which have been a permanent feature of Scotland's uniquely layered judicial system. Whilst Progress treasure’s Britain’s Common Law tradition, we also must ask reasonably why underperformance is still ongoing.

It is abundantly clear that when state capacity exists, and that all institutions are interlocking with each other in concert, that wonders can truly be achieved. One clear example is Scotland's often under-appreciated crime reduction strategy of the early devolution era, especially in Greater Glasgow, which was the murder capital of Europe at the time (London now has up to 1.7x the crime rate of Glasgow). This isn’t merely a question of throwing intense resources at a singular problem, but ensuring institutions are fully optimised to work together. We saw the start of this approach, especially when it came to policing and health in Glasgow - but progress has stalled.

### CR2. Energy

**Specialist Doc**

**Overview**

Ordered priorities: Cost, cost, cost, security, reliability, carbon.

Key result: Lowest industrial energy costs in the G7 by 2035.

To be achieved by:

- Massively increasing domestic generation
- Rapid deregulation of nuclear power.
- Emergency projects, supported by legislation and a dedicated mission-focused team (similar to the Vaccine Taskforce in conception and urgency) to bring new reactors online within 3 years.
- Infrastructure investment and deregulation to make use of existing generation and facilitate further non-government-supported installation.
- [Remainder Available in Full Policy Pack]

**Priorities**

We must focus relentlessly on energy prices. While retail prices are important for campaigning, industrial energy cost has a larger overall impact.

Currently, Britain has the highest industrial electricity prices in the world: 50% higher than our near neighbours in Germany and France, and 300% higher than the USA or Canada. These costs are paid in everything we make, buy and use, and are driving businesses from steelmakers to pubs into bankruptcy.

But it’s worse than that - it’s not just an economic headwind. Energy costs are a key driver of inflation (particularly over the last 5 years), and a huge amount of government expenditure, from the triple-cap and public sector salaries to our Chagos reparations, is indexed directly or indirectly to inflation.

But, somehow, it’s even worse than that - it’s not just bankrupting the country. By 2030 the issues won’t be critical: they will be existential. The extraordinary power that will accrue to nations able to control AI will make the ability to fund and power domestic datacentres as vital as vaccine access was during Covid. It may be that by the time we win the election we are already too late, but we must plan for the scenario in which we still have time to fight and win. If we cannot power our own datacentres, we cannot hope to stay alive.

Other concerns - particularly security of supply and reliability - are important for similar reasons, but will be guaranteed as part of actions taken primarily to reduce industrial energy costs (namely a massive expansion of nuclear baseload). Similarly, carbon budgets (if they are to be maintained at all) will be reached as a consequence of our nuclear expansion.

To come:

- Specific changes to nuclear regulation (ALARP, Justification, etc)
- Recognition/equivalence for overseas regulators
- SMR programme support
- Removal of and/or partial transfer of levies from electricity to gas
- Renewables Obligation, FiT surcharge, CfD levy, Capacity Market charge.
- Fund via carbon market revenues + general taxation.

## Our Policy Plan, Against the Spectrum

Do not talk about our position on the political spectrum. We do not have one.

We are a big-tent politics party – we design policies to benefit everyone, not just our pet voter groups.

If you want to understand how this is possible – that is, if you want to receive a vision of the next evolution of politics, based on in-depth understanding of real voter needs and a willingness to serve them impartially – read below.

### Economy

- Unicorn farm economy plan is globally unsampled, non-spectrum position
- Our SEZ policy is, in one sense, pure unencumbered free market (right-coded), but will also be motivated by centrally planned winner-picking and industry subsidy (left-coded); it will be geared towards radically improving skill capital in selected areas (classical liberalism), while also being partly driven by international/MNC investment (neoliberalism)
- Massive focus on skills and the bargaining power of labour is left coded
- Our return-of-paid-overtime and incentives to make all employees partners in a company is nominally left-coded, but is aimed at increasing collective stake in society, long-term job and financial stability, and even improving TFR, which are right-coded
- Revising union structures so unions have dual responsibility to both employee and company, pushing for upskilling through skill badges while pushing also for automation, is a non-spectrum position, makes no sense to anyone but the voters

### Crime

- Our stance on blue collar crime and high-speed prison building will be further to the right than the Tories. Our stance on white collar crime will be more daringly left-wing than Labour have dared to be in generations.
- Compounding sentencing for recidivism.
- Illegal migrant detention.
- Prosecution of benefits cheats.
- Our championing of open justice - “You can see from the last several years that the government lies to you about crime: how much of it happens, who commits it, and which public figures are complicit in it, in the name of ‘cohesion’. We believe all information about crime can be, should be, must be made open.” - will appeal to all who are not themselves pro-crime.

### Civil Service Reform

- Civil service pare back, eliminating huge numbers of civil service jobs, opening up competing agencies to phase out scrofulous/inept existing institutions, is nominally right-coded
- Enaction of the LFG Bill; Rolls Royce SNRs in every pollity and Oklo/Enron egg fusion reactors thereafter;
- NHS-free-at-point-of-purchase is left-coded, where decentralisation of NHS services and reduction in head-count/administrative density is right-coded
- 10 year legally enforced to-nil reduction on low-skilled migrant labour
- Support for the disabled, made all the simpler by our policies for finding and prosecuting benefits cheats
- Support for veterans
- Support for early years care/new mums
- Support for preventing and otherwise ending porn addiction among young men. "Mum, Dad: you are losing your boys, and your boys are losing themselves. Here's how we'll help you get them back [outline issue area, policy for education, benefits etc]"
